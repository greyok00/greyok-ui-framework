Notes on Reduction

What the kiln letterheads taught us about taking things away — transcribed from the curator's talk, with the pressroom's citations attached.

Transcribing · 4 of 6 sections

A reduction is not a removal. When the northern kilns asked for a letterhead in 1989, the studio gave them back three sheets: one with everything, one with half, and one with only the line. The kiln chose the line — and then asked for smaller.

Reduction in print has a physics. Ink sits on paper the way water sits in a channel: give it less room and it reads darker. Every artifact in this archive got quieter between its first and final proof, and the record cards remember the stages. We keep the failures too — they are the argument, written in the margin.

“Take the frame off. The paper is already the frame.” — Miho Sato, proof puller for the moon calendar series, 2003

The rule we still measure by: a finished sheet has no element a viewer cannot name in one breath. The 2011 moon calendar has a line, a disc, and a date — and the date is debatable, which is the point.
