How the ring rises

Eighteen meters of dome, one live pipe organ, and a cue sheet written for the person pulling the lever.

Reading time 7 min

The trick of a planetarium is that nothing should look like a machine. The ring of fire that opens "Ring of Fire" is drawn by eleven projector pairs and timed by a person breathing.

The operator warms the manual starball bulb at 18:45, forty-five minutes before the first ticket scans.

Operator path: Ctrl+Alt+L locks the lamp, Space walks the cue list.

# house-to-starball handoff, act IIcue 18: dim house 2.4s, hold 1.2sstarball.warm("manual bulb", lamp=0.72)wait house.quiet # usually 40 s

The three beats

  1. The warm-up. Lamps run at 72% until the house settles; the dome should feel like dusk, not noon.
  2. The handoff. Between acts, the operator dim-bounces from projector grid to starball without a visible seam.
  3. The exhale. After the finale, house lights come up at 2.4 seconds — slow enough to keep the sky in your eyes.
"We rehearse the dark." — Cue sheet margin, undated, in pencil
