Order of operations

A poster leaves this building only after it passes the same ladder it did in 2019. People ask why we still write the run sheet by hand. The answer is on the wall: MK-088, the run where a firmware update ate the counter halfway through pull 200. Pencil does not update.

The jig is the whole trick

Registration is a fixture called the T-2. Set the two pins, square the palette, check at pull twelve. Miss the check and the black pass drifts a half-millimetre by pull 250 — you can see it in the door stamp if you know where to look. The counter on press one is a mechanical tally; you zero it with Shift + 0 and hear the click in the shop. The press firmware's own counter lives behind MENU → COUNTERS → RESET and we leave it alone.

What the counters say

Sheets per hour sits at 260 on a good day. The dryer is the bottleneck: eleven posters fit on the rack and each takes nineteen minutes to cure. Two meters, printed on the run sheet every hour, so the pull rate never outruns the cure rate.

Why the hall matters

The press runs inside a show hall because posters and shows feed each other. The Friday crowd watches a run finish at 6:40 and buys the edition at 6:58. Nobody who has watched a squeegee pull a three-color registration buys the digital print from the merch table in back.

  • Thursday — art locked, screens burned, floor swept.
  • Friday — ink mixed at 07:00, pulls start at 08:00, hall opens at 19:30.
  • Saturday — the flat file restocks, the numbered COAs go in the drawer.
