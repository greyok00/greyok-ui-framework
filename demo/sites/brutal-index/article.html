Step one — the deed, not the boxes

A deposit begins when the agreement is signed and the signatories are keyed to the seal list. Boxes may sit in transfer custody for months; the deed is what makes the rows searchable. Every signatory pair is recorded as DEPOSITOR → SIGNATORY, and the index will not read SEALED rows without both.

Amendments are versioned, never overwritten A change to a deed is a new edition of the aid. The index keeps the old reading for pulls made under it.

The survey, in the tool

Surveyors work in the accessions app, one folder at a time. The keyboard does almost everything: N for next folder, R to flag a restriction, Ctrl+S to write the row. The shelf slug is mechanical — AISLE-SHELF-BOX-FOLDER — and the app refuses to write a row without it.

The night lane

Indexing happens at night, when the reading room is empty and the shelves are still. The lane writes the aid into the catalog, assigns shelf slots, and prints the box labels — it never reads sealed content. The morning shift finds the finding aid in the catalog and the shelf already labeled. Disputes go to me, and I have lost every dispute that cited the log.

  • Deed day — the seal list is updated first, before anything moves.
  • Intake day — custody photos bind the boxes to the accession number.
  • Night lane — index writes and prints; nobody is in the room to disagree.
