production notes · no 7

How an episode is made, in nine rooms and one porch

From a napkin at the Belmont coffee shop to a mailed sticker sheet: the whole route a Bumblefluff short walks before anyone's kid sees it.

1 · The board 2 · Nine rooms 3 · The numbers 4 · Sources

Every Wiggleworks episode is six minutes, nine rooms, and one porch. The porch never moves. Here is the path a short takes, with the numbers from "The Peach Protocol," which aired as the season three opener.

1 · The board

It starts with the storyboard wall. Twelve panels, pinned crooked on purpose — we rotate one at random each pitch meeting so nobody gets precious. A panel earns its spot if a five-year-old at the test table laughs before the beat is even animated. "The Peach Protocol" killed its original panel 5 because the test table voted, 6 kids to 0, that Bomble should be late differently[1].

Panel 5 died so that Bomble could arrive via the flower box instead of the door. Best edit of the season.

Dialogue passes in the same room on Thursday. Actors record standing up, because sitting voices are, in our director's words, "grocery-store voices." The mix desk is ancient and held together with a sticker from episode 12.

2 · Nine rooms

The studio is a converted tile shop on Belmont, so the rooms have names like "the grout room" and "the showroom." Each episode passes through nine of them. The pipeline is tracked in Sticker Board and the run of the whole season currently looks like this — press the keys to jump a room in the shot list:

# shot list keys — Sticker Board bind "g" -> grout room # pencil test bind "shift+g" -> showroom # color pass bind "b" -> the porch # set lock, never moved

Most of the animators work with a shared shortcut deck, the kind that sticks to the desk: space shift+G B ⇧+↑ — flip between pencil test and color of the same frame without touching the mouse. The sticker on the space key has been there since 2019.
