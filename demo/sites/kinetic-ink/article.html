The delivery standard: how a Kinetika cut leaves the building.

BS By Bram Sallow, delivery lead · 6 Nov 2026 · 9 min read

A finished title sequence is not a file — it is a stack of reviewable states. This is the standard every Kinetika cut passes through, and the reason our slots hold when other studios’ slip.

Frames, not promises

When a client asks “when do we see it?”, the honest answer is a frame range, not a mood. We schedule the week so that every day ends with something on the desk: a board, a timing pass, a graded plate, or a wrapped master. Nothing stays in your machine overnight.

The standard has four gates boards · animatic · final · delivered, and each gate produces a numbered cut. The numbering is boring on purpose — westbound_v11 tells you exactly where you are.

The desk as a instrument

Playback is muscle memory. The desk keys match the edit bays our clients grew up in:

J K L shuttle · ← → step one frame · ⌥ + click drop a note pin on the frame.

A note pin is better than a paragraph: it points at frame 00:00:22:04 instead of “the part after the second light sweep.” Vague notes cost a render cycle, and renders are the money.

$ kin pin export westbound_v11 \
    --range 00:00:22-00:00:25 \
    --format edl --marker note_pins \
    → wrote 7 pins to westbound_v11_notes.edl
