The close: a five-day checklist

Read time 9 minutes · applies to accrual books with 1–3 bank feeds

A close is not a flurry of last-minute entries. It is a short, boring ritual that happens the same five days every month. Done right, Day 5 is signing a document you already believe. Here is the checklist we run for every client ledger, and the receipts for why each day exists.

the argument

Ritual beats memory

The ledgers that close fastest are not the simplest; they are the most ritualized. Same days, same order, same sign-offs. Variance review only opens accounts that moved more than 2%, which is typically six accounts, not sixty.

41 h to 27 h

The five days

  • Day 1Reconcile every bank feed to the statement. 94% matches on arrival; the rest are suggested, not guessed. Target: unreconciled rows = 0.
  • Day 2Post recurring accruals (depreciation, prepaids, payroll accrual) and true-up anything the feed found. Print the journal. journal printed
  • Day 3Variance review, account by account. Anything that moved over 2% gets a note in the ledger itself, not an email.
  • Day 4Second-person review. The reviewer signs the journal print. Two sets of initials, one page.
  • Day 5Owner signs the certification. Invoice the month. Archive the workpapers. books certified

The Day 3 shortcut: variance bands

Fifty accounts at any detail level is a two-day job. Bands make it an hour. The rule we teach: 2% of the account, or $500, whichever is bigger. Under band, you initial it and move on. Over band, you write one sentence: why. The sentence is the workpaper.

Why the print matters

The screen is for entering; the paper is for certifying. A journal page you can fold and hand to a banker does more for trust than any dashboard. Fernbank's ledger desk keeps a running journal.pdf that renders the print without print styles — one click, on Day 2, Day 4, and Day 5.
