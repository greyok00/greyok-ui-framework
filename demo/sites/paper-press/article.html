MINE DESK · EDITION C · PAGES 1, 8–10

The last cage down

RA BY RUTH ALDERTON · photographed by E. Dahl · 11 minutes to set corrected once copy desk held section 7

The cage drops at five-forty, the same minute it has dropped since 1912, and tonight it drops for the last time. Twelve men ride it. Nobody speaks until the depth marker for 400 feet passes the window, and then the hoist operator, who has called every ride in a voice like a bell, says what he always says: “Going down.”

The Copper Bell has employed this county’s grandfathers and its grandsons with the same patience. It paid for the standpipe, half the fire line, and every gymnasium floor between here and Sunnyslope. What happens to a town when the shift whistle stops is not an abstraction here; it is an arithmetic of breakfast tables.

We rode the final shift with a notebook and a borrowed lamp, by permission of the office that signs our field permits. The mine desk has covered this company for 112 years, and we file tonight with the same rule we keep for rodeo photographs: everything named is everything checked.

Correction, Edition B Monday’s edition said 820 miners on the final payroll. The Copper Bell says 812; one man was on vacation. We correct our own paper, not the mine’s.

At 5:52 the cage returns to the surface empty. The hoist operator leans on the brake for an extra second, the way you would hold a door.
