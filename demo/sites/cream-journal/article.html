The Slow Post

EV EVAN VOSS · with marginalia by the reader of Despair, Pennsylvania

A letter that takes nine days to arrive carries nine days with it. This is not a delay; it is the letter’s luggage. The envelope rides a slower mail, sleeps in the back of a blue van outside Flagstaff, changes hands at the regional dock, and by the time it lands on the kitchen table it has been travelling with more than words.

We started the Review in 1987 on a hunch that the container is half the content. A quarterly arrives with seasons folded inside it. The quarterly that reaches you in June was being gathered in the rains of March; its margins remember the print room’s cold; its ink sat in plates that will never run again in the same combination.

Reading slowly is not slow reading. It is reading that leaves room in the day for the sentence to keep working after the eyes have moved on.

Slow readers are not slower. Ask any compositor: the fastest hands in the shop belong to the people who read proofs twice, once for sense and once for the press.

The marginalia this quarter comes from a reader in Despair, Pennsylvania — a real place, we checked — who annotates her issues with a carpenter’s pencil and mails them back to the shelf for the next subscriber's margin. Her notes now run 61 issues deep.

The last two pages are print-only We print the essay’s coda on the fold-out leaf; it exists only on paper. Subscribers get it with issue 147 in the post.

In the app the essay carries its own furniture. Press ⌘K to call the desk, / to search the margin, . to clip a line to your log. In the printed copy the same furniture is a pencil and a folded corner.
