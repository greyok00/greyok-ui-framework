Underpainting: how a mural gets from a truck sketch to 62 feet.

The Lexington Ave. grain elevator wall, told exactly as it is happening — pencil study first, gouache pass second, and a paint schedule kept to the bucket in Sketch Ledger.

See the scaled study

Every wall starts in the truck. Not a metaphor — the actual truck, parked at the site with the engine off, drawing until the light changes. This page is the honest running record of wall five: what's drawn, what's scaled, what's on the lift.

1 · The study

The elevator study lives in the ledger as entry 112-F. It is 8×24, one pass, marker green with charcoal shadows. The client saw it on a Thursday and approved it on the same Thursday, which never happens, and the revision round logged in Sketch Ledger was zero[1].

Draw the wall in the light it will be seen in, not the light you happened to be standing in.

Studies are drawn in the truck because the street is the real frame. The fence-post folio came out of the same habit; four of those studies are on the shelf right now[2].

2 · Scaling up

The grid goes on in pencil at 8.25 feet per study foot. The palette is fixed before the lift opens — four colors, each with its bucket, each bucket labeled in the ledger so the paint order is never a mystery:

# elevator wall — palette, ledger entry 112-F ground "cream-stock" #2 buckets primer, roller first shadow "charcoal" #1 bucket underpainting pass leaf "marker-green" #2 buckets main pass brass "field-brass" #0.5 bucket trim only

On the lift the same grid walks the wall in Procreate's desktop cousin — no, honestly, it is tape and chalk lines, plus one shortcut list taped to the scaffold rail:

chuck + line for the grid, bucket for ground, brush × 3 for the green. The tape measure goes on a carabiner with ⇧ nothing else — keys fall into grain bins, which is a ledger entry we do not want to write twice.

3 · Painting it

As of this weekend the wall is 62% painted. The two honest meters below are the wall's own numbers — paint coverage ticked in the ledger each evening, and the study-to-wall transfer percentage the gridding process tracks[3]:

elevator wall · 62% · west face done, north face open
grid transfer · 7 of 10 study columns · brass trim pending

Weather window closes Friday. Underpainting wants four dry days; the ledger's paint schedule flexes to Monday if the forecast breaks.

And the part nobody sees on the finished wall: the pencil grid stays. Every mural carries its drawing the way a sketchbook page carries its smudges — the studio considers that the difference between a mural and a print glued to a wall.
