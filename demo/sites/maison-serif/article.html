Care of the house silk is a winter occupation

By Constance Auberon-Lenoir · the pattern room · 14 October 2026 · an eight-minute read

The silk that leaves rue de Sévigné is finished, but it is not inert. Twill that has been hand-rolled, dyed against a 1911 recipe and rested on the bolt for a month still wants air, dark, and time. The house asks clients to care for it the way the dye room does: slowly, in the same order, with the same patience.

Three rules hold for every piece in the current book, and they are written on the card sewn into each hem:

  1. Rest after wear. A scarf worn on Tuesday rests until Saturday, hung, never folded. The twill recovers its hand within two days.
  2. Dark and dry, never airtight. The pattern room keeps bolts in unlined maple cabinets; silk sealed in plastic loses its loft within a season.
  3. No scent, ever. The 1911 dye bath is still soluble — perfume reopens it, and the colour walks.

Reading the hem card

Every plate carries a woven label and a hem card. The card gives the recipe number, the dye-bath season, and its care line. The numbers are typed against the archive ledger, so a card from 1931 and a card from this autumn read the same way:

REC 07-1931 · TWILL CHAMPAGNE · R + S = rest two days, no fold

For the desk’s ledger software, the same line is encoded in the binding table the archivist keeps; the format has not changed since the first computerisation in 1994:

# ledgers/recipe-binding — kept format since 1994 piece = ("No. 7", "champagne twill", REC=07, BATH=1931) care = if worn: rest(days=2, fold=False) shelve = maple, unlined, dark & dry

How the house keeps what it weaves

Provenance at Maison Auberon is a set of physical records, not a database claim. Each assertion below is backed by a paper in the archive and can be read during any archive consult:

  1. 1911Étiennette Auberon opens the house — first bolt woven at Lyon, recipe REC-01.
  2. 1931Recipe book bound — the champagne twill enters the kept book as Plate 07.
  3. 1968Bordeaux faille recipe — written for the opera coats of the Rive Gauche years.
  4. 1994Ledger computerisation — the binding format above is adopted; the paper book continues.
  5. 2019Mill race bought outright — the last weaving dependency leaves the house’s books.
  6. 2026Autumn Salon, the 115th year — running at 34% bench load; the desk still writes by hand.
