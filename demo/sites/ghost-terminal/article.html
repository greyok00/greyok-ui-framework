Persistence: the scroll that remembers

3.0-rc4 2.9 (still fine) 2.x EOL Mar 2027

Terminal scrollback has always been a lie you lose on exit. Waxlamp 3.0 makes it a log. This piece explains the store, the 400 ms commit window, and what happens when two machines write the same scroll — the one place we had to choose a winner.

The store

Every frame the shell commits writes a segment to an append-only log under ~/.waxlamp/store. Segments are 4 KiB, content-addressed, and replayed on boot before the first prompt. No lockfile, no fsync storm — one fsync per segment, roughly every 400 ms at human typing speed.

# what a boot replay prints (abridged)
replay store/segments 1284 segs · 71,204 lines
  marks 3 restored · cwd ~/werk/waxlamp
  -- cold start 18ms --

The 400 ms window

We commit on a timer, not on every keypress. A power cut buys you up to 400 ms of silence — the last few keystrokes. We think that trade is correct: fsync-per-key turned the 2.x prototype into a heater, and losing a partial word beats burning 4% CPU all day.

Recovering a terminal should feel like relighting a lamp, not restoring a database.

Two machines, one scroll

Sync is outside the store. Two hosts replay their own tails and wl sync reconciles by wall clock, newest frame wins, marked CONFLICT inline. We chose boring over clever because scrolls are cheap and clever is expensive to debug.

How to verify

Three steps, run anywhere: wl store verify walks the segment hashes; wl store gc --keep 30d trims; wl replay --dry times a boot without touching the live tube.

One process, one log, no daemon

The shell, the dash and the store live in one process on purpose. Anything that needs a second process has to make its case, and so far nothing has.
