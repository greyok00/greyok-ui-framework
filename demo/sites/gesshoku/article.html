Fieldnotes from the eclipsed hour

What the room ledger taught us about ending on time — transcribed from the keeper's talk, with citations attached.

Transcribing · 2 of 3 sections

Ending is the hard half of a sitting. The room keeps one rule against every temptation to continue: close the page while it still reads well. The eclipsed hour — the dark strip between nine and ten — is where that rule is practiced out loud.

Attention behaves like lamplight. Left on at full wick it burns the room out; banked too early it leaves pages unread. The ledger shows what actually happens when the wick is lowered on schedule: the note written at 20:52 and the note written at 21:34 are the same length, and only one of them was kept.

The bell is not the end. The bell is the thing you agree to in advance so you cannot argue with yourself later. — room ledger, spring of the ninth year

So the room runs on two lights. The bright hour for the work itself; the dark one for nothing at all. Between them sits the pause we keep in the margin, timed by the pattern on the page below.

The room's keys

Two keys are written beside the timer, both still obeyed — they are what the bells are made of:
