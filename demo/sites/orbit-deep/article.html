Anatomy of a pass

What actually happens between horizon raise and handover, written for the operator who just joined the night shift.

Reading time 9 min

A pass is 43 minutes long and most of it is quiet. The drama is compressed into four decisions, and every one of them has a keyboard macro.

Raising a 70-meter beam waveguide by hand is a two-person ritual. The first minute is spent confirming the ephemeris ingest, the next at

Keyboard path: Ctrl+Alt+T then K to arm the track macro.

# horizon-raise acquisition, tuned for BWG 70 mdef track(station, pass_id): if station.elevation > 5.0: # degrees station.acquire("two-way coherent") return station.downlink(limit=80)

The four decisions

  1. Arm before rise. Track macros are armed at T−08:00, not at AOS.
  2. Trust coherent. Two-way coherent resolves range and Doppler in one session; the loop-back is free physics.
  3. Watch the margin. Anything above 10 dB is breathing room. Below 6 dB, the render is lying to you.
  4. File the residuals. The archive grows by operator honesty more than by telemetry.
